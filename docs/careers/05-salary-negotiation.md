# Salary Negotiation for IAM Roles

## Why negotiation matters

Salary negotiation is the single highest-ROI career skill. A successful negotiation at the start of a career can compound into hundreds of thousands of dollars over a lifetime. Yet most candidates accept the first offer.

The data is clear: candidates who negotiate receive higher compensation than those who do not. The gap is typically 5-15% of base salary, and often more for roles with high demand and low supply, such as IAM.

## Research: know your market value

Before negotiating, research the market rate for your role, experience level, and location.

### Salary research sources

- **Levels.fyi**: tech company salaries, filtered by role and level. IAM roles are often listed under "Security Engineer" or "Identity Engineer."
- **Glassdoor**: company-specific salaries. Search for "IAM Engineer" or "PAM Engineer" at target companies.
- **LinkedIn Salary**: LinkedIn's salary insights, filtered by role, location, and experience.
- **Payscale**: general salary data. Less precise than Levels.fyi but useful for smaller companies.
- **Hired.com**: salary data from hiring platforms. Includes IAM-specific roles.
- **Blind**: anonymous salary discussions. Search for IAM compensation threads.

### What to look for

- **Base salary**: the fixed annual compensation.
- **Bonus**: performance-based variable compensation.
- **Equity**: stock options or RSUs (Restricted Stock Units).
- **Benefits**: health insurance, retirement contributions, PTO, learning budget.
- **Total compensation (TC)**: base + bonus + equity. Compare TC, not just base.

## The negotiation process

### Step 1: Get the offer in writing

Always request a written offer before negotiating. The written offer should include:
- Base salary
- Bonus structure (target and metrics)
- Equity (number of shares/options, vesting schedule)
- Benefits (health insurance, retirement, PTO)
- Start date

### Step 2: Express enthusiasm, then ask for time

Respond to the offer with enthusiasm and a request for time to review.

```
Thank you for the offer. I am excited about the opportunity to join the team and contribute to the IAM program. I would like a few days to review the details and discuss with my family. I will get back to you by [date].
```

### Step 3: Prepare your counter

Based on your research, prepare a counter-offer. Your counter should be:
- **Higher than your target**: leave room for negotiation.
- **Justified by data**: cite market rates, your experience, and the value you bring.
- **Specific**: ask for a specific number, not a range.

### Step 4: Negotiate

Schedule a call with the hiring manager or recruiter. Use the following script:

```
Thank you for the offer. I am very excited about the role and the team. Based on my research and the value I bring, I was hoping we could discuss the compensation. The market rate for this role in [location] is [range], and given my experience in [specific skill], I was hoping for a base salary of [target]. Is there flexibility on this?
```

### Step 5: Evaluate the response

The employer may:
- **Accept your counter**: great, you got what you wanted.
- **Counter with a lower number**: evaluate whether it meets your minimum.
- **Decline to negotiate**: evaluate whether the offer is acceptable as-is.
- **Offer non-salary concessions**: more equity, more PTO, signing bonus, remote work.

### Step 6: Get the final offer in writing

Once you reach an agreement, request a final written offer with the updated terms.

## Negotiation tactics

### Anchor high

The first number mentioned in a negotiation tends to anchor the discussion. If you are asked for your salary expectations, give a number at the top of your target range.

### Use silence

After making your counter-offer, stop talking. Silence is uncomfortable, and the other party may fill it with a concession.

### Focus on total compensation

If the employer cannot move on base salary, negotiate on other components: bonus, equity, PTO, learning budget, remote work, relocation assistance.

### Use competing offers

If you have multiple offers, use them as leverage. Be honest but strategic.

```
I have another offer at [company] with a base salary of [amount]. I prefer your team and the role, but the compensation gap is significant. Is there any flexibility to close the gap?
```

### Never negotiate against yourself

If the employer asks for your current salary, deflect. Your current salary is not relevant to the value you bring to the new role.

```
I would prefer to focus on the value I can bring to this role and the market rate for the position, rather than my current compensation.
```

## Evaluating offers

### Total compensation calculation

Calculate the total compensation for each offer:

```
Total Compensation = Base Salary + Bonus + Equity Value + Benefits Value
```

Equity value depends on the company's stage:
- **Public company**: equity value = number of shares × current stock price.
- **Private company**: equity value is uncertain. Discount by 50-90% depending on the company's stage and likelihood of liquidity.

### Non-salary factors

Consider factors beyond compensation:
- **Learning and growth**: will you learn new skills? work with experienced mentors?
- **Career trajectory**: does this role lead to your next career goal?
- **Work-life balance**: hours, PTO, remote work flexibility.
- **Company stability**: is the company financially stable? growing?
- **Team and culture**: do you like the team? does the culture fit your values?

## Regional negotiation considerations

### United States

- Negotiate aggressively. US employers expect negotiation.
- Equity is a significant component of total compensation, especially at tech companies.
- Benefits (health insurance, 401k match) are standard and expected.

### Europe

- Negotiation is less aggressive than the US. Employers may be surprised by aggressive counter-offers.
- Benefits (pension, healthcare, vacation) are often more generous than the US.
- Equity is less common, especially at non-tech companies.

### Gulf

- Negotiation is expected, especially for senior roles.
- Housing allowance and flights are standard benefits.
- Tax-free salary means your take-home pay is higher than the nominal salary suggests.

### Egypt

- Negotiation is common, especially at multinational companies.
- Benefits may be limited compared to international standards.
- Remote work for international companies can significantly increase your effective compensation.
